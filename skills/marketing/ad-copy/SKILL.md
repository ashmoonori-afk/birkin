# Ad Copy

Write short, punchy ad variants (headlines and body) that lead with the customer's problem, state the offer, and close with a single unambiguous CTA.

## When to Use

- Creating ad variants for Google, LinkedIn, Facebook, or email campaigns.
- Testing multiple hooks or value propositions.
- Refining a campaign's messaging before spending.

## When NOT to Use

- Landing pages or detailed product copy (use landing-copy skill instead).
- Strategy or positioning work; ad copy assumes offer and audience are set.

## Always establish first

`target customer`, `problem`, `offer`, `channel` (Google, LinkedIn, email?), and `CTA` action. If missing, state explicit assumptions.

## Procedure

1. Start with the customer's acute pain or desired outcome in one line.
2. State the offer and the #1 benefit.
3. Create 2–3 variants with different hooks (problem-focused, benefit-focused, urgency-focused).
4. Each variant: headline (30 chars for Google; longer for LinkedIn) + 1–2 lines of body.
5. One clear CTA per variant ("Learn more," "Start free," "Book a demo").
6. Cut redundant words; every word must justify its space.

## Output

- 2–3 ad variants, each with headline + body + CTA.
- Channel-specific lengths and tone (Google vs. LinkedIn vs. email).
- Mark any missing proof or assumptions.
- Suggested A/B test hypothesis (e.g., "problem hook vs. benefit hook").
