# Email Draft

Compose an email that respects the reader's time, gets straight to the point,
and makes the ask obvious so they know what you need.

## When to Use

- Cold outreach, pitches, or partnership inquiries.
- Status updates, requests for feedback, or follow-ups.
- Need to convey bad news or disagreement professionally.

## When NOT to Use

- Internal chat or Slack messages (email is more formal).
- Long-form narratives (break into multiple emails or use blog-post skill).

## Procedure

1. Identify **who**, **what**, and **by when** in one sentence before drafting.
2. Write a subject line that signals value or urgency (not spam-like).
3. Open with context (why you're writing, credibility, or reference).
4. State the ask in one clear paragraph, not buried.
5. Provide one brief supporting reason or benefit to them.
6. Close with a specific next step (reply by date, click link, schedule call).
7. Keep length under 200 words; aim for 3–5 short paragraphs.
8. Call `write_file` to save as email.md or email-draft.md.

## Output

- Plain text or markdown (.md) email ready to copy and send.
- Includes subject line, body, and optional signature.
- Tone: direct, professional, respectful of time.
